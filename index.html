What goes on your slab

A floor coating is built, not painted.

Most garage floors in town were poured plain gray and left that way. A few summers later they carry tire marks, drip stains, hairline cracks and a film of fine dust that never sweeps up. Coating fixes all of that, but only if the concrete underneath is ready to hold it.

That's where Paradise Valley Epoxy spends most of its time. We look at the slab before we quote it, checking for old sealer, oil soaked into the surface, moving cracks and moisture from below. Select a layer to see what it does.

The right system for the room, not one system for every room.

A shaded casita, a west-facing garage and a pool deck on the same property can each need something different.

Some floors want a fast-cure polyaspartic because the garage door stays open half the afternoon. Some want a thick epoxy body under a clear topcoat. Outdoor slabs need texture and colors that stay cooler underfoot. A few floors don't need a coating at all, and when that's the case we'll say so.

Choosing a chemistry

Epoxy, polyaspartic and polyurea, side by side.

All three are resin floor coatings, and a good system often layers two of them: an epoxy or polyurea base for bond and build, with a polyaspartic clear for sun. This is the general picture. Individual products vary.

Why coat it

Five things change when the slab is sealed.

A resin coating cures into one hard skin bonded to the concrete, with no joints or grout to catch dirt. The build can be thick or thin to suit the room.

A brighter room

Light colors and gloss bounce light around a deep garage or shop.

Less dust

Sealed concrete stops shedding the powder bare slabs give off.

Spills wipe up

Oil, coffee and pool chemicals stay on top instead of soaking in.

Your colors

Flake, solids or metallics chosen to match cabinets, stucco or stone.

Holds up to sun

UV-stable topcoats keep color near open doors and on patios.

Project sequence

Seven steps from walkthrough to parked car.

This order holds for nearly every job. Well over half the labor happens before a single drop of color touches the floor.

  1. Visit

    We walk the floor with you, note cracks, stains and old sealers, and ask how the space is used.

  2. Scope

    You get a written scope with the system, colors, prep and a realistic schedule.

  3. Grind

    Diamond tooling with dust extraction opens the concrete so the coating can bite.

  4. Repair

    Cracks, chips and low spots are filled and ground flush.

  5. Build

    Primer and body coats go down, plus flake or metallic pigment if chosen.

  6. Clear

    A clear wear coat sets the sheen and texture and takes the daily wear.

  7. Hand over

    We walk you through the cure dates for walking, storage and parking, and how to clean.

The question that matters

Most coatings that peel fail at the bond, not the coating.

Two quotes can name the same product and still describe very different jobs. Ask each contractor what they do to the concrete before the first coat goes down.

  1. Mechanical profileDiamond tooling cuts a rough texture the first coat can physically grab.
  2. Clean concreteSealers, paint and oil stains are removed so nothing sits between resin and concrete.
  3. Sound surfaceChipped edges and cracks are rebuilt so the coating has solid concrete beneath it.
  4. Moisture checkVapor rising through the slab can lift a coating from below.

Kit versus pro install

Why store-bought kits often peel.

A hardware-store kit can freshen a lightly used floor. The trouble usually shows the first summer, when hot tires and sun test a thin film with a weak bond.

Removing a peeling kit means grinding all of it off, so a failed DIY floor often costs more to fix than a professional job would have cost at the start.

Day-to-day care

Simple habits for a floor that lasts.

A sealed slab asks for much less attention than raw concrete or grout lines. A few easy habits keep the shine and protect the clear layer.

  • Sweep out the grit

    Fine granite and wind-blown sand act like sandpaper underfoot. Dust-mop or soft-sweep once or twice a week.

  • Use a neutral cleaner

    Warm water and a pH-neutral cleaner are plenty. Harsh acids, citrus degreasers and vinegar can take the shine off certain clears.

  • Wipe drips early

    Brake fluid, coolant, pool acid and battery leaks should come up the same day, even on a resistant floor.

  • Pad the pressure points

    Use pads under jack stands, kickstands, ladder feet and the legs of gym racks.

  • Roll, don't drag

    Move cabinets, toolboxes and furniture on dollies so sharp edges never scrape the finish.

  • Respect the cure

    Foot traffic, storage and vehicles each have their own wait. Your cure sheet spells out each date.

Questions we hear most.

Send your question
What does a garage floor coating cost in Paradise Valley?

Price depends on square footage, how much grinding and repair the slab needs, which layers go down and the finish you choose. A five-bay garage with a lift bay is a very different job from a two-car garage, and a slab with old paint costs more to prep than bare concrete. We give a firm written price after seeing the floor.

How long will a coated floor last?

That comes down to prep, the system and how hard the floor is used. A diamond-ground slab with a full-build system and a proper topcoat lasts far longer in a home garage than a thin kit. Heavy commercial traffic wears any floor faster, so we size the wear layer to the use.

Our garage door faces west. Will the color hold up?

Yes, if the top layer is UV-stable. Many epoxies amber in strong sun, so on sun-exposed floors we finish with a polyaspartic or another light-stable clear. The base under it can still be epoxy.

What causes hot-tire pickup, and how do you prevent it?

It happens when a thin or poorly bonded coating softens under a hot tire and pulls off the slab. Diamond grinding for a strong bond, a full-build system and waiting out the full cure before parking are what prevent it.

Can you coat around a car lift, a golf cart charger or floor drains?

Yes. We detail around lift posts, drains, water heater stands and charger mounts, and finish edges cleanly at walls and door seals. Point them out at the visit so we can plan the edges.

We're away for the summer. Can you work around our schedule?

Yes. Many clients here split the year between homes. We can meet you or your house manager for the visit, then schedule the install while you're away and send photos when it's done.

Can you work with our builder or house manager?

Yes. On new builds and remodels we coordinate with the general contractor on timing and slab testing. On existing homes we're happy to work through a house or estate manager.

Is a coated floor slippery when wet?

A slick gloss finish gets slippery when wet, and monsoon rain tends to blow right in at the garage door. Flake floors have natural texture, and we can add fine grit to the clear. Pool decks and patios always get a textured finish.

What do we do with everything in the garage?

The floor needs to be completely empty for grinding and coating. Owners often use a portable storage container, the driveway under tarps or another room for a few days. We'll tell you exactly when things can go back.

How soon can we park again?

It depends on the system and the temperature. Fast-cure polyaspartic systems often allow foot traffic within hours and cars within a few days. Epoxy-based systems take longer. Your cure sheet lists the dates.

We're building new. When can the slab be coated?

New concrete has to cure and release moisture before most coatings bond well. On new homes and additions we test the slab and schedule the coating when it's ready rather than going by the calendar.

Will the coating hide cracks?

We fill stable cracks before coating, and a flake pattern makes them hard to see. Cracks that are still moving because of soil or slab shifts can reappear, since the coating moves with the concrete.

Can you put a coating over tile, pavers or paint?

Our systems are for concrete. Paint and old sealers are ground off first. Tile and pavers generally aren't suitable without removal, and we'll tell you plainly if a surface won't work.

Is the estimate free?

Yes. We visit, look at the slab, talk through options and send a written estimate. The rough size of the space and a few photos with your request help us prepare.
